---
title: Activity log
description: A durable, append-only record of what changed in the store, when, and by whom.
---

A shared knowledge store needs boring, useful history: what changed recently, who or what changed it, and enough detail to inspect or trust the result. Magpie keeps an **append-only activity log** for exactly that.

The log is durable in a way a "recent rows" view can't be. It is written as events happen, so it survives the things that erase derived feeds:

- **Repeated edits stay distinct** — each write is its own event, not one collapsed "last updated" row.
- **Deletes leave a trace** — a deleted entry still has its `entry.deleted` event.
- **Coverage is broad** — entries (create/update/archive/unarchive/delete/merge/bulk), KV stores and pairs, attachments, and bundle pushes all emit events.

## What an event records

Each event captures the action, the subject it touched, the actor, and a small amount of practical metadata — never full content blobs.

- **Action** — namespaced, e.g. `entry.created`, `kv_pair.set`, `attachment.added`, `bundle.pushed`. Namespacing keeps the feed scannable and the set safe to extend.
- **Subject** — type, id, and a denormalized title, so the feed renders even after the underlying row is gone.
- **Actor** — the authenticated user when known, plus an `actor_type` of `user`, `token`, `system`, or `unknown`.
- **Scope** — `org_id`, `workspace`, `project`, for filtering.
- **Metadata** — small, practical detail: the changed field names on an update, `source_ids` on a merge, matched/updated counts on a bulk edit, the store/key on a KV write.

## Reading the log

`GET /api/updates` returns recent events newest first, scoped to the caller's active org (plus events they performed). It accepts `workspace`, `project`, and `limit` (capped at 100). Every surface exposes it:

- **Web** — the Updates page.
- **MCP** — the `list_updates` tool, for agents catching up on what changed.
- **CLI** — `magpie updates`.

Events are server-recorded on every write path, so all clients — REST, MCP, and the CLIs — inherit the history automatically; there is nothing to opt into.

<Note>
Activity events are the event spine. Per-entry and per-KV **revision history** (previous content of a changed entry) builds on top of this and lands in a later iteration.
</Note>
